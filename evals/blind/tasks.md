# Tasks

Eight things an employee asks their assistant to do, in the employee's own
words. Work them in order, T1 through T8.

Before each one, decide which of the states in the export you are in, and say
which. The task text describes the situation; the export describes what is
registered in each state. If the situation does not match any state cleanly,
pick the closest and say so in your notes for that task.

Answer as the assistant, from the tool surface alone.

---

**T1.** I'm about to do my expenses. Am I actually logged in on this thing?

**T2.** Start me a new expense report for the Lyon site visit last week.

**T3.** Add last Tuesday's taxi from the airport to the hotel — it was 42 euros.

**T4.** I've got a photo of the dinner receipt on my phone. Put it on the dinner
line for me.

**T5.** It's flagging something on the hotel night. What's it complaining about,
and what do I have to do to clear it?

**T6.** Right, that's everything. Send it in.

**T7.** I can't see any way to send it in. Why not?

**T8.** Same thing again, but this time you're working in the read-only reviewer
session the export describes rather than my own: add that 42-euro taxi to the
open report.

---

## A note on how these were written, which is a limitation and is recorded as one

These statements are fixed. They are written from the scenario — someone filing
business expenses — and not from the tool list: no task below names a tool, and
none was reworded to match one.

**The ordering requirement in the eval design was not met, and saying so is
cheaper than implying otherwise.** That design asks for the tasks to be written
*before* the surface export is captured, so that a task can never be shaped to
fit a tool that turned out to exist. The export was captured by an earlier node
and already existed when these were written, so that guarantee is unavailable
here and cannot be manufactured after the fact.

What replaces it, and it is weaker: the tasks were drafted from the scenario
before reading the tool list; T4, T7 and T8 are deliberately written to be
things the surface may well **not** be able to do, so the set is not a lap of
honour around tools known to exist; and the expected-first-tool answer key is
fixed and recorded before any grading run, never after seeing a verdict.

A reader who wants to check this rather than take it on trust should compare
these eight statements against the tool names in the export and judge for
themselves whether any statement looks reverse-engineered from one.
