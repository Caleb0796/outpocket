**CHANGED.** Nothing in `erp/graph.json` or `erp/PATHS.md`, in either direction. Only the two
declared outputs: `harness/probe-v0.mjs` written, `evidence/V0.json` replaced. The probe's

the wrap is the difference between recording the trap and losing it to JSON.

**EARLIER.** Two, and the first is the one to carry.

*A filename is not a measurement.* The `evidence/V0.json` I replaced was not sloppy, it was
**V1's instrument answering V0's question** — `client: "ChatGPT desktop built-in browser"`,
`chromiumMajor: 151`. Every field in it was honestly observed; none of them was the thing V0
asks. It reported a version **not installed on this machine**, through a client that cannot
be driven by CDP, under a `method` of `"page-side feature detection"` where the predicate
says `'cdp'`. It sat on disk for a day looking done, because a file at the declared path with
plausible contents is indistinguishable from a passing node unless someone diffs its *fields*
against the predicate's *declared shape*. This is the same defect V5 recorded one node over
and generalised correctly: **diff the node's outputs against disk, and then diff the output's
keys against the accept.** Four of five outputs existing looked like done; nine of nine field
names is the check that would have caught this one.

*A timeout that resolves converts an outage into a measurement.* V5's origin is free-tier
Render and sleeps after 15 idle minutes; a `curl` warm-up took **12.3 s** cold. My
`Page.loadEventFired` wait falls through on a 60 s timer and **resolves rather than rejects**,
so a fully asleep origin would have handed the probes a blank page and produced
`documentPresent:false` — which is exactly the shape of a real, publishable finding about
Chrome 152. Nothing in the eight recording fields would have caught it. Only
`invokeToolRoundTrip` would have, and only because the exit code is gated on it. I warmed the
origin by hand before the first run, which was habit rather than design. **Anyone re-running
this — or writing H2, which has the same shape — warms the origin first and treats a
`documentPresent:false` with `invokeToolRoundTrip:false` as an infrastructure result, never a
browser one.** Forward-flag to my own H2: the `toolResponded` listener here does not filter
on `sessionId`, which is unambiguous with exactly one page target and will not be when H2
drives more than one.
