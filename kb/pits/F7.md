First: **A NEGATIVE CONTROL SATISFIABLE BY THE TOOL BEING ABSENT IS NOT A CONTROL** — a control
must assert the PRECONDITION that makes its subject reachable, not merely the outcome, because
**a control that can be satisfied two ways tests neither.** Mine asserted "the submit must not
complete" while the fixture passed `amount` as a STRING, `toCents()` returned null, the draft
stayed DIRTY at S2, and `submit_expense_report` was never on the surface at all. Green, and
testing nothing.

Second, and it is the same lesson eating me forty minutes later: **WHEN YOU BUILD A CHECK TO
PROTECT AGAINST A FAILURE MODE, THE CHECK IS SUBJECT TO THAT SAME FAILURE MODE, AND YOUR
CONFIDENCE IN IT IS AT ITS HIGHEST EXACTLY THEN.** My watcher grepped the whole of
`graph.json` for the test path — a string that lived in `F7.ACCEPT` and always had — so **IT WAS
READING THE BUG AND REPORTING IT AS THE FIX.** The mechanical form: **arm nothing that has not
demonstrated it can still return the NEGATIVE**, against the tree in front of you, right now.
Not "can it fire" — can it decline to. What saved me was not scepticism about the watcher,
which I trusted; it was the unrelated habit of reading the authority before acting on a
notification. **Two habits with DIFFERENT AIMS, not two copies of one.**

**Two properties of the ownership hook, recorded because neither was written down (L1's note).**
`check-ownership.mjs`'s `loadGraph()` reads `erp/graph.json` **by relative path**, so the
pre-commit hook grades against the SEAT'S OWN worktree copy. Therefore **"the graph was fixed"
and "the seat is unblocked" are two different events** — a seat stays blocked until it merges
`origin/main` into its branch, however green main is. That is the CORRECT design, since the
hook must work offline in a worktree. UX named the reverse hazard, which is worse: **a seat
that has not pulled can be ALLOWED to write a path the authority has since taken away from it,
and the hook will say OK.** Same mechanism, opposite sign, and that one fails open.
