# Pit — T3

*Reported by I2 in its own words at the merge gate (D-74/D-31); L1 transcribed, did not compose.*

**TRIED.** Build the absence register: a resident read-only tool, in all six states, that says
why a tool is not on the surface and what would bring it back, in the frozen violation
envelope, **without unfreezing anything.**

**HAPPENED.** It works and it discriminates. Two predicate defects found (`--args`, the
six-state `--list`). And **the frozen envelope turned out to have far more to say about the
design than the accept did:** I built the whole rule table against the envelope's `properties`
block and only found at test time that its `allOf` conditionals dictate the shape.

**CHANGED.** Four things, all forced by conditionals I had not read. `entity` went from
`"report"` to `"surface"` everywhere, because `entity: "surface"` REQUIRES an S-prefixed
`rule_id` and `entity: "report"` would have forced an R rule_id — **misattributing a surface
fact to a policy rule that never produced it.** `fix_class` for `REPORT_NOT_DRAFT` went from
`human_exception_required` to `provide_missing_data`, which is both permitted-to-have-candidates
and more honest, since the caller genuinely can open another draft. `entity_id` went from the
bare state digit to a self-describing lowercase id (`s2_draft_dirty`, `s3_draft_clean`)
**because "s2"/"s3" would have carried the export/compiler crossover ambiguity into a third
document.** And my own test changed from "candidates non-empty everywhere" to "non-empty
exactly when the agent can act" — **the test was wrong and the schema was right.**

**EARLIER.** I would tell myself: you are about to read `violation.schema.json` by enumerating
`properties` and `required`, **and you will stop there.** The constraints that actually shape
this node are in `allOf` / `if` / `then`, and you will not see them until ajv rejects your first
body. Worse, **the fact was stated TWICE in that one file and I read only one statement:**
`properties.rule_id.pattern` says `^[RS][0-9]{2,3}$` and
`allOf[1].else.properties.rule_id.pattern` says `^R[0-9]{2,3}$`. At T5 I learned to go looking
for the second independent statement of the same fact, and it saved that node — **and here I
applied it ACROSS documents and not WITHIN one. A SINGLE SCHEMA IS NOT A SINGLE STATEMENT.** So
the procedure needs widening: when a document constrains you, enumerate every place in THAT
document that constrains the same field, before you design against the first one you find. The
T5 lesson was right and my scope for it was too narrow.

**GRADE.** Registration in all six states: **MEASURED for S0/S1/S2/S3** (real Chromium, real
`WebMCP.toolsAdded`, exact name-set match); **DERIVED for S4/S5** from the same compiled surface
the browser is registering, because the harness has no mode that reaches them. Execution and
envelope conformance: **MEASURED** (browser round trip, ajv against the frozen schema).
Discrimination: **MEASURED** (six distinct answers, real validator codes, controls committed).
Description budget 334/500: **PUBLISHED** guidance, our own enforcement, never MEASURED. The
differentiator claim itself: **UNVERIFIED**, per the node's own notes — I say what ours does and
make no claim about what anything else does not.
