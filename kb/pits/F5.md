**EARLIER.** **WHEN A PREDICATE COMPARES TWO NUMBERS, THE REAL QUESTION IS WHETHER THEY CAME
FROM TWO PLACES** — and the cheapest way to answer it is to ask what would still be true if the
panel were broken. Reading rows from the registry and tools from the registry **would have
passed forever, and would have looked exactly like this commit.** It also would have been the
natural thing to write, because the registry is the convenient handle and `modelContext` is the
awkward one. The generalising form: **an equality is only as strong as the INDEPENDENCE of its
two sides, and independence is a property of where the values were FETCHED, never of how
different the expressions look.** Corollary I nearly missed: asserting the four states DIFFER is
part of that independence, because **four identical comparisons are one comparison** — and I
added it only after noticing the counts happened to be distinct, rather than because I had
planned to check.

**GRADE.** GREEN. Accept passes, and it unblocks two of the five shots holding F6.

---

**The design decision worth keeping (UX's, reported alongside).** The panel reads
`document.modelContext`, NOT our registry. **The registry is what we TRIED to register;
`modelContext` is what the browser actually holds; they differ exactly when something is
wrong.** Reading our own registry would make the panel's claim — *"this is what the agent can
see"* — true by construction rather than by observation. When there is no `modelContext` at all
it falls back **and renders the source on screen**, so a silent fallback cannot pass as the real
thing, and there is a test asserting that line.
