# Pit — F1

Reported by **UX** under D-31 across two rounds, transcribed by L1.

**TRIED.** Branched from main and read `erp/contracts/session.contract.md` as the authority
rather than `server/index.mjs`, per the DO line. Ran `tools/accept-gate.mjs F1 --list` FIRST, on
the L0 pit's advice, which split the predicate into two spans and made it immediately visible
that one is a harness call and the other a DOM assertion — worth doing before writing a line,
because the two have completely different blockers.

Round two: merged main first (the worktree predated both the static route and `--smoke-login`),
**reproduced the failure verbatim through `accept-gate` before forming any theory**, then READ
`harness/drive.mjs` to find out how the click is actually delivered rather than trusting a
summary of it. Confirmed the mechanism by measuring the page before changing a line, then
instrumented `window.fetch` to COUNT POSTs so "no double-fire" was measured rather than asserted.

**HAPPENED.** Span 0 exits 0, 2/2, **via the `dom` path and not the `fetch` fallback** — so what
was under test was the shell, not the server. Span 1 counts exactly 2 in three states: signed
out, after a button login, after a card login.

**The mechanism was not the one in the lead L1 gave me.** The handler already used `closest()`.
The real defect is that **`closest()` walks UP the tree while the `<button data-login>` sits
BELOW the click**, inside the `<li data-persona>` — so `[data-login]` is structurally unreachable
from a click on the card, however it is dispatched. Measured rather than reasoned:

    card box                              443 x 185
    elementFromPoint at the centre        LI  (the card itself, not a child)
    hit.closest('[data-login]')           null
    hit.closest('[data-persona]')         "chen"
    li.contains(button)                   true
    button.contains(hit)                  false

The centre lands on the LI's own padding, so no child is involved at all. The inference that a
child was swallowing the click was close but is not what happens — which is why the symptom
matched the prediction exactly.

**CHANGED.** Two files, both declared F1 outputs: `src/page/ui/shell.js` gained the card-level
fallback branch (the `[data-login]` branch is checked FIRST, so a button click never falls
through), `src/page/index.html` gained cursor and hover so the enlarged target reads as
clickable. Nothing in `server/`, `harness/` or `tests/`. **I did not edit the failing test to
make `npm test` green, which is the move that was available and wrong.**

**EARLIER.** The lesson is about what my Day-2 verification PROVED versus what it appeared to
prove. I wrote then that I had verified login "through the real click path" — and I had, but on
a page whose layout put the button under my cursor, **because I clicked the button**. So I
tested "the shell can log in" while span 0 tests "the page a judge drives logs in". Those differ
by exactly one assumption — where the click lands relative to the handle — and my rig could not
see the difference because **the rig and the shell shared the assumption**. The generalisable
form: **a delegated click handler encodes a claim about the click's position relative to its
target, and you cannot test that claim with a click you chose the position of.**

It also sharpens my earlier note rather than repeating it: the near-miss where the header nearly
took `data-persona`, and the click that could never reach `[data-login]`, are the SAME defect
class from two sides — cosmetic-looking DOM decisions that silently falsify a predicate about
the page a user actually drives, and neither shows up in any test that drives the page by its
ids instead of its pixels.

Second, smaller: **I nearly accepted a diagnosis handed to me.** It was directionally right and
would have produced a fix that worked, and I would never have learned that `closest()` was
already there. Measuring the page cost two minutes and turned a plausible story into a mechanism.

**GRADE.** GREEN, and honestly so — both spans measured, the fix verified through the real driver
on the real path, no gate worked around and no file touched that was not mine.
